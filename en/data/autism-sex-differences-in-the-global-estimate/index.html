Autism: sex differences in the global estimate

WorldData from 2021DRAFT

Draft page. The sources are named and linked; final verification is still pending.

The GBD 2021 global model estimated 1,064.7 autistic people per 100,000 males and 508.1 per 100,000 females. These are modelled epidemiological estimates and are not equivalent to the proportion of diagnoses recorded by health or education services.

The figures

1,064.7 per 100,000age-standardised prevalence in males
508.1 per 100,000age-standardised prevalence in females

GBD 2021 estimated a modelled prevalence approximately twice as high in males as in females.

What it measures

The comparison uses age-standardised rates from the same global model. It is useful for observing an estimated difference between males and females and, when compared with higher diagnostic ratios, for showing that identification and prevalence are not exactly the same thing.

Diagnostic ratios are often higher than this modelled difference. A review of 54 studies and more than 13.7 million participants found that the male:female ratio was 4.20:1 when all studies were pooled, but fell to 3.25:1 in studies that screened the general population instead of starting only from already existing diagnoses.

This is compatible with identification bias: diagnostic figures do not measure prevalence alone.

How to cite it

Always state that the sex-specific rates are age-standardised modelled estimates. Do not present them as a global diagnostic ratio or as a closed biological difference.

What to avoid

The figure must remain linked to its population, territory, year and method. Separating it from that context can change its meaning.
