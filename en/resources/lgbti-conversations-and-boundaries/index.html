Preparing a conversation about name, pronouns, privacy and boundaries

You can prepare only what you want to communicate now: what treatment you are asking for, what information you want to keep private, and which channel is easiest for you to use.

This tool does not assume that you want to disclose your identity or orientation. It also does not require a personal explanation in order to use it.

1 · What I want to ask for

2 · Who can know

3 · How I want to communicate it

It can be in person, by message, email, form or note. Written communication can give you time to organise the information and keep a record of what was agreed.

You do not have to choose the hardest format in order to prove anything.

4 · Short phrases to adapt

  • “Please use the name ___ for me.”
  • “You can share this with ___; please do not share it with other people.”
  • “I do not want to explain more about this now.”
  • “If you make a mistake, correct it and move on.”
  • “I need this change in writing.”

Before sharing personal information

If you are concerned that the conversation could affect your safety, housing, money, care or access to services, you can limit what you share, choose another time, use a written channel or ask a trusted person to be present. This page does not replace legal advice or specialist support in a situation involving violence or discrimination.

Legal framework consulted · Spain

In Spain, Law 15/2022 includes sexual orientation or identity and gender expression among the protected grounds in equal-treatment and non-discrimination law. Law 4/2023 establishes the national framework for the real and effective equality of trans people and the guarantee of LGBTI rights.
