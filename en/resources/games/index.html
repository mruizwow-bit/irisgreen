Resources

Games and routines

Choose by life stage, context or what you want to practise. These are practical supports for organising, sequencing, planning or practising everyday situations; you do not need a diagnosis to use them. No timer, no points and no rush.

Create or download a visual routine

You can use these resources in childhood, adolescence or adulthood. They are not diagnostic tests and you do not need to choose a condition before you start.

You need JavaScript to play. These are the games:

  • Shoelaces, step by step. Put the steps for tying your laces in order.
  • Put the shower in order. Place the shower steps, from first to last.
  • What is missing to brush your teeth?. Look at the sequence and choose the missing step.
  • What comes next? · morning. The morning has started. Choose what comes next.
  • Pack your backpack. Put what you need today in your backpack.
  • Dress for the rain. It is raining. Choose clothes that protect you.
  • Object and action. Match each object with what you do with it.
  • Before and after eating. Put what happens at the table in order.
  • The odd one out in the shower. Everything is for the shower except one thing.
  • Build your morning. Choose your steps and put them in your order. You can print it.
  • Night checklist. Tick off what you have done before bed.
  • Route to the bus. Place the stops on the route, from home to where you are going.
  • Zips and buttons. Two short sequences: doing up a button and a zip.
  • Which comes first?. To have cereal, what do you do first?
  • Two routines mixed up. Sort what is for brushing teeth and what is for making the bed.
  • Where does this go?. Take each thing to its place in the home.
  • A rainy morning. The usual morning, with rain. Put the steps in order.
  • Put the washing in order. From dirty clothes to clothes on the line.
  • Set the table. Choose what goes on the table for a meal.
  • The odd one out in the shopping. Everything is food except one thing.
  • Crossing safely. The steps to cross the street calmly.
  • Waiting your turn. Put what happens in a queue in order.
  • Pay and put away. The steps to pay in a shop. No sums.
  • The weekend bag. You are away for two days. Choose what to take.
  • Before sleep. From screen on to lights off.
  • Build your afternoon. Plan your afternoon. Include a break.
  • First, then. Choose what you do first and what comes after.
  • What is missing when you go out?. You have almost everything. What is missing?
  • Put the sandwich in order. The steps to make a sandwich.
  • Tidy the room. Choose where each thing goes. Some fit in two places.
  • The route to the doctor. What happens from leaving home until you are calm again.
  • Opening a pack. The steps to open a tricky pack.
  • Morning checklist. Tick off what you have done before leaving. You can print it.
  • Each thing and its place. Match each place with what is kept there.
  • The countdown. A clock that empties little by little. Start when you want.
  • Dress for cold, heat and rain. Three different days and one wardrobe.
  • What comes next? · night. The evening has started. Choose what comes next.
  • Sort for recycling. Take each thing to its bin.
  • The odd one out on the desk. Everything helps you study except one thing, which can wait.
  • Start with one. To start a task, choose a small piece. There are several good ones.
  • Medication reminder. Tick each time of day once you have done it.
  • Going out with the dog. Put the steps of the dog walk in order.
  • Changing activity. Moving from one activity to another, choosing how. Both options work.
  • Coming back from a break. Getting back to the task without rushing, starting small.
  • Shopping and paying. Pick up what is on the list, queue and pay with no sums.
  • Getting ready for a medical appointment. The practical side: what you take and what you do while you wait.
