“Noise is unbearable for them”

Sensory experiences

This page describes an everyday situation, not a diagnosis. The same situation can have several explanations, and a professional assessment is what distinguishes between them.

In brief

In the playground, supermarket or a family meal, the person covers their ears, leaves or becomes upset, and from the outside it can look as though they went from zero to one hundred. The environment may have been building up sound for a long time beforehand.

It does not automatically mean

  • An intense sensory experience does not establish autism or a sensory disorder on its own.

What to look at

  • Where it happens and where it does not, and what is different: echo, crowds, music, a hair dryer or building work.

What may help now

  • Reduce avoidable noise in the environment before asking for anything else: turn off background television, close a door or move to another room.

What to avoid

  • Reading ear-covering as rudeness or fussiness.

When to seek professional help

Seek an assessment if the discomfort is new, persistent, painful or substantially limits daily life, or if specific symptoms involving hearing, vision, skin, headache or another possible medical cause appear.

Warning signs

If sudden hearing or vision loss, a very severe sudden-onset headache, weakness or a speech change, or difficulty breathing with swelling of the lips, mouth or throat appears, seek urgent medical attention.
