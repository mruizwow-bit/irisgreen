Technology and assistive products

WorldData from base 2021–2022; proyección 2050DRAFT

Draft page. The sources are named and linked; final verification is still pending.

More than 2.5 billion people need at least one assistive product. Nearly 1 billion lack the access they need, and WHO estimates that global need will exceed 3.5 billion people by 2050.

The figures

More than 2.5 billionpeople need one or more assistive products

These products include physical and digital supports: hearing aids, glasses, mobility aids, prostheses, speech recognition, time management, captioning and solutions for communication and cognition.

Almost 1 billion

of people who need them do not have access.

Between 3% and 90%

was the observed access range in country surveys included in the global report, reflecting enormous inequality.

3.5 billion in 2050

WHO projects that the number of people who will need assistive technology will increase to 3.5 billion in 2050.

What it measures

WHO and UNICEF measure both the need for assistive products and actual access. The category includes physical products and digital solutions for communication and cognition, so the gap is not limited to mobility or hearing.

WHO’s definition of assistive technology expressly includes digital solutions for cognition and communication, so this block connects directly with AAC, visual supports, time management and accessibility.

How to cite it

Distinguish need for assistive technology, lack of access and future projection. Do not present 3.5 billion as a current figure.

What to avoid

The figure must remain linked to its population, territory, year and method. Separating it from that context can change its meaning.
