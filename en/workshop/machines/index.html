The workshop · Studio 9

Machines studio

Real simple machines. Build gear trains and see how fast and which way each one turns; move the pivot of a lever; choose pulleys to raise a tonne; plan a chain machine step by step.

  • Gears, lever and pulleys
  • Live calculation
  • Table and text for each part
  • Printable chain machine
  • 18 challenges on 5 levels
  • Motion only when you ask

How it works

  1. Choose a machine above the drawing, or a challenge.
  2. In Gears, add parts from the panel: beside another one, or on the same axle. Mark in the table which one is the motor and which one the output.
  3. In Lever and Pulleys, change the values in the panel and see how much force you need.
  4. In Chain machine, add steps and write what each one does. The studio tells you if a step does not fit the one before.
  5. Challenges check themselves. Save the project to a file or print the list of steps.

The tool needs JavaScript. Here are all the challenges so you can do them on paper or with another tool.

Challenges

The challenges are grouped by level. You can start with any of them.

Level 1 · First mechanisms

  1. Three times slower. Build two gears so the output turns three times slower than the motor.
  2. Same direction, same speed. Make the output turn as fast as the motor and in the same direction.
  3. Lift 60 kg with 20 kg. Move the pivot, the load and the place where you push until you lift 60 kg with a force of 20 kg at most.
  4. Half the effort. Raise 40 kg with a force of 22 kg at most.
  5. A chain of three. Plan a three-step machine where each step starts with the movement the one before ends with. Write what each one does.

Level 2 · Force and direction

  1. Four times the force. Make the output have at least four times the motor’s torque (turning force).
  2. Three times faster. The output must turn three times faster than the motor and in the same direction.
  3. Like a wheelbarrow. Build a second-class lever: pivot at one end, load in the middle and force at the other end. 60 kg with 30 kg or less.
  4. Like tweezers. Third-class lever: the force goes between the pivot and the load. Move 10 kg with no more than 25 kg of force.
  5. 100 kg with under 30. Raise 100 kg with a force under 30 kg.

Level 3 · Compound trains

  1. The hour hand. On a clock, the minute hand goes round 12 times while the hour hand goes round once. Build that train: 12 times slower and in the same direction.
  2. Sixty to one. From seconds to minutes: the output goes round once for every 60 turns of the motor, with no gear over 60 teeth.
  3. Little force and little rope. Raise 50 kg to a height of 2 m with under 20 kg of force and pulling no more than 8 m of rope.
  4. Six steps, four parts. A machine of six steps that fit together, with at least four different kinds of part.

Level 4 · Heavy loads

  1. Half a tonne. With a 3 m plank, lift 500 kg with a force of 50 kg at most.
  2. One tonne. Raise 1000 kg to 3 m with 250 kg of force or less (for example, four people) and 20 m of rope at most.

Level 5 · No ceiling

  1. Surprise ratio. A different ratio comes up each time. Build a gear train that matches it exactly. “Another ratio” gives you a new one, endlessly.
  2. The great machine. Ten or more steps that fit together, with at least six kinds of part. Then, if you like, build it for real with what you have at home.

How it calculates

Gears: all of them have teeth of the same size (module 2 mm), so their size depends on the number of teeth. Two gears that touch turn in opposite directions, and the speed changes in inverse proportion to the teeth: 20 against 60 turns three times slower with three times the torque. Gears on the same axle turn together.

Lever: the force times its distance to the pivot equals the load times its distance to the pivot.

Pulleys: the load is shared between the lengths of rope that hold it, but you have to pull more rope. A 5% friction loss is counted for each pulley and 0.4 kg for each movable pulley.

  • It is a model for learning: it does not replace the calculation of a real machine or the safety rules for lifting loads.
  • Speeds are given in turns per minute (rpm). Torque is given as a multiple of the motor’s torque.

Your work and your files

What you make stays in this browser tab. It is not sent anywhere and the website does not keep it.

To carry on another day, press “Save file”: a file with your project downloads to your device. To pick it up again, press “Open file” and choose it. If you close the tab with unsaved changes, the browser warns you.
