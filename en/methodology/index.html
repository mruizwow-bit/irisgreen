Methodology

How the information here is checked, rule by rule, with an example from this website for each one.

Iris Green is not a healthcare professional. What is here is documentation: if a diagnosis or treatment is needed, that happens in a consultation.

How the information here is checked

Five rules, each with an example you can open.

1 · Every claim has its document, its year, and what that document covers and does not cover

Example. NICE guideline NG69, from 2017, covers anorexia, bulimia, binge-eating disorder and OSFED. It does not cover ARFID or pica, so those two pages do not cite it.

View the condition pages

2 · Pages without a source say so on the page itself

Example. The dissociative amnesia page says that treatment evidence is scarce: no document is named because there is none, not because it was overlooked.

View a page with the notice

3 · Figures with their denominator: who was asked, how many people and by which method

Example. The four autism figures in the data collection do not measure the same thing, and each one gives its population and method in the same sentence.

View the data pages

4 · Certainty grades separate what exists from what helps

They are two different questions and are answered separately. A page may have the main box at grade A and the treatment at grade C.

  • Grade A. It is supported by a clinical guideline or a systematic review, named and dated.
  • Grade B. It is supported by an institutional document or individual studies, with their limits stated.
  • Grade C. It is reasonable practice, not a research finding. The page says so in those words.

View the grades in the pages

5 · The review date is visible, and there is a notice when a guideline is due to be replaced

Example. CG31 is current and under update. The page says so and includes the date when it needs to be checked again.

View the review work

How it is written

  • No one is singled out and no one is labelled. The official names of a diagnosis or a procedure are kept because they are needed to request them, never as the definition of a person.
  • There are no derogatory words, not even to challenge them: writing them already puts them into the reader’s head.
  • There is no advice disguised as evidence. When something is reasonable practice and not a research finding, the page says so.
  • Short sentences, one idea per sentence, and an accessible reading panel on every page.

What each thing is and is not

  • An everyday situation is not a diagnosis. Every situation page says this visibly.
  • A screening scale does not diagnose.
  • A correlation does not prove causation.
  • A lived experience is not a prevalence figure.
