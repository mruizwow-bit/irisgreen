“At work I use all my energy trying to look as though I'm fine”

Daily life

This page describes an everyday situation, not a diagnosis. The same situation can have several explanations, and a professional assessment is what distinguishes between them.

In brief

The work gets done, and it gets done well. What is left out is everything else: shopping, a phone call, dinner, seeing someone. The working day is paid for in the hours afterwards, and that cost may not show up in a performance review.

It does not automatically mean

  • Performing well at work does not rule out a barrier or a high cost outside working hours.

What to look at

  • Which part of work uses the most energy: meetings, the open-plan office, the phone, people nearby or last-minute changes.

What may help now

  • Request adjustments linked to the specific barrier: headphones, camera off, meetings with an agenda, a seat against a wall, or remote work on high-contact days where appropriate and available.

What to avoid

  • Proving you can cope by maintaining a pace that is paid for at home.

When to seek professional help

If work is being maintained at the persistent cost of basic activities, rest or social life, it is worth reviewing workload, health and possible adjustments. In Spain, workplace adaptation may form part of reasonable adjustments where applicable.

Warning signs

If there is a major deterioration in health, self-harm, suicidal thoughts or inability to meet basic needs, seek help without waiting.
