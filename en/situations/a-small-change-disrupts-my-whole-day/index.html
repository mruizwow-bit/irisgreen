“A small change disrupts my whole day”

Education and work

This page describes an everyday situation, not a diagnosis. The same situation can have several explanations, and a professional assessment is what distinguishes between them.

In brief

The time or place of an appointment changes, and what falls apart is not only that hour but the sequence that had already been built around it. From the outside the change looks small; rebuilding the plan may not be.

It does not automatically mean

  • Difficulty starting, sustaining, switching or finishing a task is not enough on its own to conclude ADHD, autism or lack of ability.

What to look at

  • What is disrupted by the change: only that hour, or also transport, food, recovery time and what comes afterwards.

What may help now

  • Ask for advance notice and, when possible, ask for it in writing; a message can be easier to use than something mentioned in passing.

What to avoid

  • Announcing a change and expecting an immediate response.

When to seek professional help

Seek an assessment or specialist support if the difficulty is persistent, appears in several settings and substantially limits education or work. If ADHD is suspected, assessment should consider the history from childhood and functioning in more than one setting.

Warning signs

A sudden new difficulty with organisation or attention together with confusion, marked memory loss, weakness, a speech change or other neurological symptoms needs urgent medical assessment.
