“I can't explain my symptoms to the doctor”

Daily life

This page describes an everyday situation, not a diagnosis. The same situation can have several explanations, and a professional assessment is what distinguishes between them.

In brief

In the waiting room it was clear what you wanted to say, but inside the consultation it comes out in the wrong order, or the minor details come out and the important part stays unsaid. You are being asked to remember, organise, summarise and decide at the same time, often within a short appointment.

It does not automatically mean

  • An everyday activity being difficult does not equal lack of autonomy and does not point to a diagnosis on its own.

What to look at

  • Whether the information comes out in a disorganised way or does not come out at all.

What may help now

  • Bring it in writing and hand it over, or read it aloud; both are valid ways to communicate.

What to avoid

  • Relying entirely on memory inside the appointment.

When to seek professional help

Seek an assessment if the difficulty is new, clearly worsening, affects several basic activities or substantially limits safety, health, education, work or independent living.

Warning signs

A sudden change in memory, orientation, speech or ability to carry out basic tasks needs medical assessment. If a person cannot meet basic needs for food, hygiene or safety, seek help without waiting.
