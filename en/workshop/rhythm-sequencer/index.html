This studio is an interactive tool and needs JavaScript. Below you will find the full explanation of how it works and what you learn.

The workshop

Rhythm and sequencer

A real drum machine. Tap the cells to add hits, change the tempo and hear the loop while you edit. Add a bass or a melody whenever you like: it is the same music studio.

What you can make here

  • Your own looping beat
  • A backing track with drums and bass
  • A WAV file to listen to and a MIDI file for other programs

How it works

  1. Press Play (or Space). The example beat plays in a loop.
  2. In the grid below, each row is a sound and each cell a part of the bar. With the Pencil, tap a cell to add a hit; tap it again to remove it.
  3. The tracks are at the top. Drag a clip to move it and its right edge to lengthen it. On the ruler, Shift + drag sets the loop.
  4. Under Properties you change the tempo, the grid, each track’s volume and pan, and the velocity of each hit.
  5. Export as WAV or MIDI from File.

How to read the grid

A bar has four beats. With the grid set to sixteenth notes, each beat is split into four cells: sixteen cells per bar. The kick usually falls on beats 1 and 3; the snare on 2 and 4.

Real time, audio clock

The playhead and the notes follow the sound card’s clock, not the page’s timers. That is why the rhythm stays tight even when the computer is busy.

What you take away

WAV is the mixed sound, ready to listen to or upload. MIDI is the score: the notes of every track, which you can open in another music program to change the instruments.

Your work and your files

What you make stays in this browser tab. It is not sent anywhere and the workshop does not store it in the browser.

To keep it, open File and press “Save project”: a file downloads to your device. To carry on another day, open File, press “Open…” and choose that file. Exports (images, sound, 3D models, games) are also files that you download yourself.

How it is made

Libraries are served from irisgreen.eu, with no outside services, and they only load in the studio that uses them. Licences: Tone.js (MIT) and PixiJS (MIT); our own AudioWorklet level meter · full list of licences.
