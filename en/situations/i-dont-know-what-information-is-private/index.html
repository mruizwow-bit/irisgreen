“I don't know what information is private”

Relationships

This page describes an everyday situation, not a diagnosis. The same situation can have several explanations, and a professional assessment is what distinguishes between them.

In brief

You share something normally and realise from the other person's reaction that it may not have been information for that setting. Privacy is not always intuitive; it can help to treat it as a classification that can be learned explicitly.

It does not automatically mean

  • A social or relationship difficulty does not establish a diagnosis and does not allow you to infer another person's intentions.

What to look at

  • What kind of information is involved: body, health, money, home life or information about another person.

What may help now

  • Use three categories: information for anyone, information for some people, and information for nobody without explicit permission.

What to avoid

  • Answering every question simply out of politeness.

When to seek professional help

Identity or orientation do not require clinical assessment. Professional or community support may be useful if there is persistent distress, discrimination, safety or privacy difficulties, violence, isolation, or if the person wants support with decision-making.

Warning signs

If there is violence, coercion, an immediate threat or a risk of self-harm, prioritise safety and seek urgent help. In Spain, the 024 helpline can be used for suicide risk and 112 for a life-threatening emergency.
