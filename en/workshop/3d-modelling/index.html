This studio is an interactive tool and needs JavaScript. Below you will find the full explanation of how it works and what you learn.

The workshop

3D modelling

Build objects by putting shapes together: cubes, spheres, cylinders, cones, pyramids, rings and wedges. A shape can be solid or a hole that cuts through the others. Everything is measured in millimetres, ready for 3D printing.

What you can make here

  • A keyring with your initial or a drawing
  • A phone stand
  • A box with a lid that fits
  • Spare parts, scale models and prototypes
  • STL files to print, GLB for the web and OBJ for other programs

How it works

  1. Choose a shape in the toolbar and press “Add solid”. It appears on the print bed.
  2. Tap a shape to select it. The coloured arrows on the model move it; “Rotate” and “Scale” change its angle and size. You can also type the measurements in Properties.
  3. To make a hole, add a hole shape and place it where you want to remove material.
  4. Select several shapes (Shift + tap, or Shift + click in the list) and press “Group”: solids join together and holes cut through.
  5. Check the “Before printing” section and export from File: STL for the printer, GLB for the web or OBJ.

Solids and holes

A solid adds material. A hole takes it away. When you group, the studio joins all the solids and subtracts the holes: that is how you make the hole in a keyring or the inside of a box. You can ungroup a group to change the pieces again.

Measurements and print bed

The grid is a 200 × 200 mm print bed with lines every 10 mm. The arrow keys move 1 mm (10 mm with Shift). “Drop to bed” lowers the shape until it touches the base: anything floating in the air cannot be printed without supports.

Which file to choose

STL is the format for 3D printers: it only stores the shape, not the colour, and here it is exported with the Z axis pointing up, as slicing programs expect. GLB stores shape and colour and opens in web viewers and 3D programs. OBJ is a text format that almost any program understands. Loose holes are not exported.

How it works inside

Drawing uses WebGPU when the browser offers it and WebGL 2 otherwise, without you having to do anything. Joining and subtracting shapes is calculated with constructive solid geometry (CSG). It only redraws when something changes, to use less battery.

Your work and your files

What you make stays in this browser tab. It is not sent anywhere and the workshop does not store it in the browser.

To keep it, open File and press “Save project”: a file downloads to your device. To carry on another day, open File, press “Open…” and choose that file. Exports (images, sound, 3D models, games) are also files that you download yourself.

How it is made

Libraries are served from irisgreen.eu, with no outside services, and they only load in the studio that uses them. Licences: Three.js (MIT) with WebGPU or WebGL 2 and three-bvh-csg (MIT) · full list of licences.
