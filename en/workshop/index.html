Iris Green · Workshop

The workshop

Create in your own way. There is no single right answer and nothing is scored.

What you make here is kept only in this browser tab and is deleted when you close it. It is not sent anywhere. Do not write personal details.

  • Lab
  • Challenges away from the screen
  • My notebook

Pick a table and start. Everything can be undone.

Lab

Without JavaScript, the interactive tables are shown as paper challenges. All the content is still here.

Table 01

Story lab

Three pieces (four in "More pieces") and a story. Change one piece and see what happens.

Example pieces: a retired lighthouse keeper, a night market, a key that opens nothing, the power goes out.

Away from the screen: Character sheet (printable A4 sheet)

Table 02

What if…?

An impossible question. Write every answer you can think of.

Examples: What if trees could talk? What if a house had no doors? What if rain came in colours?

Away from the screen: Blank sheet (printable A4 sheet)

Table 03

Invent something new

Put two things together and make an invention. Give it a name.

Example: umbrella + backpack. What is your invention called?

Away from the screen: Invention sheet (printable A4 sheet)

Table 04

Transform the shape

Simple shapes. Add, move and turn pieces until they become something else.

Challenges: Turn the pieces into an animal. Make a vehicle that doesn't exist. Build a face with three pieces.

Away from the screen: Nine-square grid (printable A4 sheet)

Table 05

Many uses

An everyday object. What else could it be used for?

Objects: a paper clip, a wooden spoon, a clothes peg, an egg carton, an odd sock, a rubber band.

Away from the screen: Collection sheet (printable A4 sheet)

Table 06

Change the point of view

The same scene told from somewhere else: another person, an object, another time or another scale.

A bus stop on a rainy day. There is a bench, a forgotten umbrella and a driver waiting for departure time. Viewpoints: The forgotten umbrella, The driver, In 100 years, An ant on the bench.

Away from the screen: Four panels (printable A4 sheet)

Challenges away from the screen

The 72 challenges from the current workshop, to do with whatever you have at home.

Drawing · 13 challenges

Ten times the same thing

Draw the same object ten times on one sheet. Change one thing in each drawing.

Ten minutes · Paper and pencil

Another way: Do it with ten photos of the same object, moving one thing each time.

Without looking at the paper

Look at the object the whole time and draw without looking at the sheet. It comes out crooked. That is the game.

Ten minutes · Paper and pencil

Another way: Draw with your eyes closed and look afterwards.

The three-part creature

Join a part of an animal, a part of a machine and a part of a plant. Give it a name.

An afternoon · Paper and colour

Another way: Cut the three parts out of magazines and glue them.

The swapped colours

Draw something familiar in the wrong colours: a blue lemon, an orange sky.

Ten minutes · Paper and colour

Another way: Do it with phone filters on a photo of yours.

Exactly as it is

Copy a trainer, a remote or a mug. With the crease, the stain and the tangled cable.

An afternoon · Paper and pencil

Another way: Draw the outline only, no details.

The same scene, three sizes

Draw the same thing three times: very small, medium and filling the whole sheet.

Ten minutes · Paper and pencil

Another way: Use three sheets of different sizes.

Fewer lines each time

Take an object from the table and draw it six times. Each time with fewer lines than before. The sixth can be a single line.

Ten minutes · Paper and pencil

Another way: Cut it out of paper six times, smaller each time.

The hand you do not use

Draw whatever you like with the hand you do not write with. It comes out different, and that is the point.

Ten minutes · Paper and pencil

Another way: Hold the pencil with both hands at once: it comes out just as wobbly, which is what the challenge is about.

What is inside the blot

Put down a blot of colour with plenty of water and let it dry. Look at it and draw over it only what you found inside.

An afternoon · Paper, water and colour

Another way: Use a blot from another day, or one on a wall, a floor or a stone.

The one-thing notebook

Pick one thing you can draw over several days: a plant, a mug, your own hand. Draw it once a day for five days, on the same sheet.

Bit by bit over days · A large sheet and a pencil

Another way: Take one photo a day and put them side by side at the end.

The object from behind

Pick something in front of you and draw its back, as if you had turned it round. Do not turn it.

Ten minutes · Paper and pencil

Another way: Turn it round at the end and compare.

One sheet, forty pieces

Fold a sheet into forty squares. Draw something different in each one. Lines count.

An afternoon · One sheet

Another way: Fill only the squares along the edges.

With the sheet upright

Tape the paper to the wall and draw standing up. The whole arm moves differently from the wrist.

Ten minutes · Paper, tape and a wall

Another way: Draw with the paper on the floor, crouching.

Writing and telling · 12 challenges

Five sentences and a photo

Take a photo of something from today. Write five sentences about it. Not one more.

Ten minutes · Phone and paper

Another way: Record yourself saying the five sentences.

The list of what does not exist

Write fifteen things that do not exist: an appliance, an animal, a school subject, a day of the week.

Ten minutes · Paper

Another way: Say them out loud and have someone else write them down.

The day told by the backpack

An object of yours tells how the day went. The object speaks, not you.

An afternoon · Paper

Another way: Record it as if it were an audio from the backpack.

Four panels with no words

A whole story in four drawings. Nobody speaks.

An afternoon · Paper and pencil

Another way: Do it with four photos put in order.

The same story, two versions

One small event told by two different people. What each one tells changes.

An afternoon · Paper

Another way: Tell them out loud and compare.

Exact instructions

Write how to tie a shoelace, step by step, for someone who has never seen it. Test whether they work.

Ten minutes · Paper

Another way: Record it on video saying each step.

Ten titles and no books

Write ten titles of books that do not exist. Titles only: the books do not have to be written.

Ten minutes · Paper and pencil

Another way: Say them out loud and record them on your phone.

What was on the table

Look at any table for a minute. Cover it with a cloth. Write down everything that was there.

Ten minutes · A cloth and something to note with

Another way: Say it out loud, or photograph it first and point afterwards.

A sentence that grows

Start with three words. On the next line, the same plus one. Keep going until it will not fit on the page.

An afternoon · Paper and pencil

Another way: Do it out loud, with someone repeating the whole sentence each time.

A hundred words and not one more

Tell something that happened to you in exactly a hundred words. Not ninety-nine, not a hundred and one. The hard part is what has to go.

Bit by bit over days · Paper and pencil, or your phone

Another way: Tell it out loud and let somebody count the words along with you.

The ending first

Write the last sentence of a story. Then write the three that come just before it.

Ten minutes · Paper and pencil

Another way: Give the last sentence to someone else and let them write the three.

A manual for something you already know

Write the instructions for something you do without thinking: tying your laces, making a sandwich, switching on the console. Step by step, skipping none.

An afternoon · Paper or phone

Another way: Give them to someone and have them follow every word.

Building · 12 challenges

The paper bridge

Make a bridge between two books that holds a third book on top. Sheets of paper only.

Ten minutes · Sheets of paper

Another way: Do it with cardboard from a box.

The shoebox

Turn a box into a small room of its own. With a floor, a light and something that moves.

Bit by bit over days · Box, cardboard and scissors

Another way: Build it with what is there without cutting anything.

The tower with no glue

Go as high as you can by stacking and slotting. No tape, no glue.

Ten minutes · Whatever is around

Another way: Build it lying down, along the floor.

Something that moves

A peg, a rubber band and a stick. Make it push, lift or launch.

An afternoon · Loose materials

Another way: Draw the mechanism and explain how it would move.

To scale

Build a small piece of furniture for a doll, a car or a figure. It has to really hold.

An afternoon · Cardboard

Another way: Do it with plasticine or salt dough.

The machine that is good for nothing

A machine with a crank, a string or a ramp whose only purpose is to work.

Bit by bit over days · Whatever is around

Another way: Draw it with all its parts numbered.

The gap underneath

Build something that holds an object above the table, with an empty gap underneath. Your hand has to fit through the gap.

An afternoon · Paper, card, tape

Another way: Use card from a box: it folds by itself and holds more.

The one-handed tower

Build as high as you can using one hand only. The other stays still.

Ten minutes · Whatever is around you

Another way: Build on the floor, with the pieces around you and no need to stretch.

A shelter for one small thing

Pick a small object. Measure it. Make it a shelter to its size, with a door it can go in and out of.

Bit by bit over days · Card, scissors, ruler

Another way: Give someone the measurements and let them cut the pieces; the building is yours.

Make it roll

Build something that rolls from the edge of a book to the floor without falling over on the way.

Ten minutes · Cardboard, bottle caps, tape

Another way: Make it roll as slowly as possible.

A house for a spoon

Build a house for a spoon. With a door, a window and somewhere to sleep.

An afternoon · Boxes and tape

Another way: Build it for two spoons that are not speaking.

The same thing, three times better

Build something simple. Take it apart and build it again. And again. Keep the three versions together.

Bit by bit over days · Whatever you have

Another way: Make the third one use half the material of the first.

Sounds · 12 challenges

Five sounds nearby

Record five sounds happening where you are right now. Let somebody else guess them.

Ten minutes · Phone

Another way: Imitate them with your mouth and have them guessed anyway.

A rhythm with three things

Pick three objects that sound different. Make a rhythm of eight beats and repeat it.

Ten minutes · Three objects

Another way: Do it with claps, clicks and feet.

One minute of street

Record one minute out of the window. Listen to all of it and write down everything you hear.

Ten minutes · Phone

Another way: Listen for one minute without recording and write it down afterwards.

Three notes

Invent a short tune using only three notes. Repeat it until you know it.

An afternoon · Voice, keyboard or app

Another way: Do it with three glasses filled to different heights.

The sound map

Draw the place you are in from above and mark where each thing sounds. It does not have to be recognisable: an invented plan works.

Ten minutes · Paper

Another way: Say it out loud, pointing with your finger.

Your recorded voice

Record a bit read or told. Listen to it. Record it again changing something.

Ten minutes · Phone

Another way: Record it without listening afterwards and keep it for another day.

The quietest sound

Stay still and look for the quietest sound you can hear right now. Give it a name.

Ten minutes · Nothing

Another way: Rest your hand on the table and look for the smallest vibration you can feel. Same challenge without sound.

The twelve-step rhythm

Walk twelve steps marking a rhythm with your feet. Do it the same three times. On the fourth, change it.

Ten minutes · Nothing

Another way: Do it with your hand on the table, sitting down.

Three sounds with a glass

A glass, a spoon and something that rattles inside: rice, coins, water. Get three different sounds.

An afternoon · A glass and a spoon

Another way: Make them with your mouth, picking nothing up.

The same minute, five days

Record one minute of sound at the same time for five days, in the same place. Listen to them one after another at the end and note what changes.

Bit by bit over days · Something that records

Another way: Write down what you hear each day, with no recording, and compare the five lists.

The same place with your eyes closed

Sit where you are and close your eyes for two minutes. Then write everything you heard, from nearest to furthest.

Ten minutes · Paper and pencil

Another way: Repeat it in the same place at another time of day.

A ladder from a single thing

Take one object and get five different sounds out of it, from lowest to highest. Record them in that order.

An afternoon · An object and your phone

Another way: Get all five from a glass of water, changing the level.

Looking · 12 challenges

The same place, ten hours

Ten photos of the same corner through the day. Lay them out in a row.

Bit by bit over days · Phone

Another way: Draw the same corner three times at three moments.

The collection of one single thing

Photograph twenty doors, twenty stains or twenty number plates on your street.

An afternoon · Phone

Another way: Draw ten instead of photographing twenty.

The one-square-metre notebook

Pick a square metre you can look at for five days: a plant pot, a bit of pavement, a corner of a park. Note what happens there.

Bit by bit over days · Paper

Another way: Take one photo a day of the same square.

The same shape in twenty places

Look for circles wherever you go: in the street, on transport, in a shop. Count them. Draw the odd ones.

An afternoon · Phone

Another way: Look for a colour instead of a shape.

Photographing the light

Do not photograph the object: photograph the shadow, the reflection or the stripe of sun on the wall.

Ten minutes · Phone

Another way: Draw only the shadow of an object.

Twenty things by colour

Gather twenty objects, sort them by colour and take a photo from above.

Ten minutes · Objects and phone

Another way: Sort them by size or by the noise they make.

Upside down

Take a photo or a drawing and turn it upside down. Look at what you see now that you did not see before.

Ten minutes · A photo or a drawing

Another way: Turn your phone round with the photo on screen.

What fits in the hole

Make a small hole in a piece of paper. Look through it and keep only what fits inside.

Ten minutes · A piece of paper

Another way: Make the gap with your fingers, cutting nothing.

The smallest thing in sight

Find the smallest thing you can see. Look at it for a good while and note three things you had not seen.

An afternoon · Something to note with

Another way: Take a close photo and look at them on the screen.

Ten times the same corner

Pick a corner of your home and take ten photos without moving from the spot. Change only where you point.

Ten minutes · Phone

Another way: Take them all at floor level.

A catalogue of hands

Photograph ten hands doing ten different things. Your own count.

An afternoon · Phone

Another way: Make sure no face appears in any of them.

What changes without showing

Pick something that takes days to change: a plant, roadworks, the light in a window. Take one photo a day for a week.

Bit by bit over days · Phone

Another way: Put them side by side afterwards.

Inventing · 11 challenges

Six squares

Invent a board game of six squares with three rules. Play it once and change one rule.

An afternoon · Paper

Another way: Dictate the rules and have someone else draw the board.

A place that does not exist

Draw the map: the coast, three villages, a river and the names.

An afternoon · Paper

Another way: Describe it out loud with all the names and write them down.

Ten new letters

Invent an alphabet of ten signs. Write your name with it.

Ten minutes · Paper

Another way: Use ten hand gestures instead of written signs.

A tabletop sport

New rules using whatever is on the table. How you win, how you lose, what is not allowed.

An afternoon · Whatever is around

Another way: Adapt a game you already know by changing three rules.

A coin

Invent it: what it is called, what it is worth and what one buys.

Ten minutes · Paper

Another way: Model it in plasticine or cardboard.

A code

Invent a way of writing messages. Send one to somebody you trust and give them the key separately.

Ten minutes · Paper

Another way: Make the code with colours instead of letters.

The second use

Take the first thing in front of you. Invent a second use for it, with nothing to do with the first.

Ten minutes · Nothing

Another way: Say it out loud and let someone write it down.

One new rule

Pick a game you already know. Change one rule, only one. Play a round with the new rule.

An afternoon · A game you already have

Another way: Change the rule of a card game: it takes one sentence to explain.

The missing animal

Invent an animal that lives in a library. Decide what it eats, where it sleeps and what it hides from.

An afternoon · Nothing

Another way: Tell it, draw it or build it, whichever works better for you.

A ten-word language

Invent a language with only ten words. Decide which ten and write three sentences with them. The interesting part is what cannot be said.

Bit by bit over days · Paper and pencil

Another way: Make them ten gestures instead of ten words.

A museum of one thing

Invent a whole museum devoted to one ordinary object. Write the names of its five rooms.

An afternoon · Paper and pencil

Another way: Draw the floor plan and mark the way out.
