Communication without speech, mutism, apraxia, stuttering and difficult speech

HealthDRAFT

Draft page. The sources are named and linked; the final check is still pending.

Not speaking, speaking little, stuttering or being unable to speak in a specific situation are different experiences. The common rule is not to confuse availability of speech with understanding or capacity to decide.

In a conversation

Give time, ask one question at a time, allow writing or pointing, do not automatically finish the sentences of someone who stutters, and do not require oral speech to prove understanding.

At school and in healthcare

Agree an alternative way to respond before the blocking situation appears: tablet, keyboard, cards, gestures, board or support person. AAC must be available even if the person also speaks at other times.

Selective mutism

Reduce pressure and allow non-oral responses. Rewarding, punishing or putting the person at the centre so that they “finally speak” increases demand and can worsen anxiety.
