10-game pilot

B1 pilot · 10 games

These are the ten pilot games used to validate the new system. They do not replace the full inventory.

B1-01

Tying shoelaces

Follow the steps for tying laces, one at a time, with no rush.

Choose the step that comes now. If you get stuck, you can ask to be shown one.

B1-02

Brushing teeth · What is missing?

Look at the whole sequence and place the step that is not there.

Look at the sequence. Choose the step that goes in the gap.

B1-03

Packing the school bag

Put into the bag what is needed today.

Choose an object and press “Add to the bag”.

B1-04

Dressing for the weather

Choose clothes that work for going out today. Several answers are good.

Choose at least two items that work for you. When you are done, press “That is it”.

B1-05

Changing activity · Plan A or Plan B

Move from one activity to the next, choosing how.

There is no rush. Choose what you need now. Both options are fine.

B1-06

The bus route

Put the steps of the bus trip in order, from home to school.

Choose the step that comes now. The map is optional: the route makes sense without it.

B1-07

Everything in its place

Choose where each thing goes. Some places work for more than one.

Look at the object and choose a place. If two fit, both are fine.

B1-08

Getting ready for a medical appointment

Organise the practical side of an appointment: what to take, how to get there, what to do while waiting. This is not clinical information.

Choose something and press “Add to the folder”.

B1-09

Shopping and paying

Pick up what is on the list and pay with no mental arithmetic.

Choose a product and press “Add to the basket”.

B1-10

Back to work after a break

Return to the task without rushing and start with something small. No streaks, no points.

Both are fine. Nobody is keeping score.

M05

Match

Before or after

M07

The one that does not help

M08

M09

Visual checklist

M14

Choose the first step

Lower the barrier to starting by choosing one small, concrete action.

M17

Observe, ask, do not assume

Separate what you can see from what you must ask before assuming.
