    Situations · Communication

    They explain it to me and two minutes later I don't know what I was meant to do

    In brief

    You may hear an instruction and forget it before using it, especially if you are doing something else. Having the steps in writing lets you look back at them.

    What may help now

    • Ask for the steps in writing

      A two-line note may be enough to check what you need to do.

    • Ask for one instruction at a time

      Instead of receiving several instructions in a row, ask first for the one you need to follow now.

    To say or show

    This page describes an everyday situation, not a diagnosis. The same situation can have several explanations, and a professional assessment is what distinguishes between them.

    In brief

    Hearing an instruction and holding it in mind while doing something else are two different tasks. Spoken information disappears; written information stays available.

    It does not automatically mean

    Working memory may be involved when an instruction is heard but is lost before it can be used.

    What to look at

    Whether it happens only with long instructions or with short ones too.

    What may help now

    Ask for the instruction in writing, even if it is only two lines.

    What to avoid

    Giving three instructions in a row while passing in a corridor.

    When to seek professional help

    Seek an assessment if this happens frequently and limits education or work. If conversations are also hard to follow in noise, words are missed or hearing loss is suspected, hearing should be assessed; if the pattern is long-standing and appears in several settings, attention, language or executive-function assessment may also be relevant.
